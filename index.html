Members of a series differ by –CH₂–. Molecules attract each other through intermolecular forces: London (dispersion) forces between all molecules, and hydrogen bonds between molecules with an –OH group.

Source 2: Measuring a boiling point

A liquid boils when bubbles of vapour form throughout it. While it boils steadily, its temperature stays constant: this is its boiling point.

Without anti-bumping granules, a liquid can heat a few degrees above its boiling point and then boil suddenly and violently. This is called bumping. A thermometer also takes time to catch up with the liquid's temperature.

Source 3: Data-book boiling points / °C at 101 kPa

Compounds with a boiling point below 22 °C are gases at room temperature. There is no alkene with one carbon atom.

Source 4: Hazards

Alkanes, alkenes and alcohols: highly flammable liquids and vapours. Never heat with a naked flame.
Methanol: toxic if swallowed, inhaled or absorbed through the skin.
Carboxylic acids: corrosive or irritant to skin and eyes; strong, unpleasant smell.
Hot oil bath: can reach 250 °C and cause severe burns.
